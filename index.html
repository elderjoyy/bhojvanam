How a partnership works

From suitable land to a living food forest

Each project is planned around the land, water availability, local climate, intended use and the institution's priorities.

01 • EXPLORE

Understand the site

We discuss the land, ownership or control, soil, sunlight, water, access, security and the institution's goals.

02 • PLAN

Agree the project

We prepare a suitable concept, planting and management approach, responsibilities, investment plan and written agreement.

03 • DEVELOP

Establish the food forest

Planting, soil improvement, water planning and essential infrastructure are coordinated with suitable local expertise.

04 • MANAGE

Care and monitor

Regular maintenance, replacement planting where agreed, crop care and progress records support long-term development.

05 • HARVEST

Record and share

Harvests are weighed or measured, recorded and allocated according to the written produce-sharing arrangement.

06 • CONNECT

Explore participation

Where the site is suitable and authorised, the partners may consider learning visits, volunteering or nature programmes.

A proposed partnership model

A shared harvest. Shared value.

In selected institutional food-forest partnerships, the agreed usable harvest may be divided equally: 50% for the institution and 50% for Bhojvanam. For example, 100 kg of recorded usable produce would be allocated as 50 kg to each partner.

Institution's shareMay support staff, students, community kitchens or other uses agreed by the institution.
Bhojvanam's shareMay be sold or distributed through suitable channels, subject to applicable food requirements.
Actual harvest onlySharing is based on the actual recorded harvest, not a promised quantity or guaranteed income.
Clear written termsCosts, responsibilities, harvest records, losses and distribution are agreed before work begins.

Who can explore a partnership?

Colleges and universities • Hospitals and healthcare campuses • Companies and corporate campuses • Government and public institutions • Residential communities • Landowners with suitable open land.

Every project is subject to site suitability, land permissions, a written agreement, agreed investment and operating responsibilities, and actual seasonal harvests. Food-forest establishment takes time; produce quantities and financial returns are not guaranteed.
